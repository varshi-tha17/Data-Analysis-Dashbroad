# Data-Analysis-Dashbroad

# CARBON CREDIT ANALYSIS 2024-2025(Interactive DashBoard Creation Using MS Excel)
# OBJECTIVE 
This project delivers a fully interactive Carbon Credit Analysis Dashboard developed in Excel, designed to support organizations in evaluating and optimizing their environmental and financial performance. The dashboard provides a comprehensive view of key sustainability metrics including greenhouse gas emissions, carbon credit utilization, compliance expenditures, and projected cost savings through a series of structured, insight-driven visualizations.

-	What is the net trading position of each industry?
-	What is the total market value associated with each industry?
-	How does each industry compare in terms of carbon revenue and carbon expenditure?

# PROJECT INSIGHTS

## Emissions vs Allowance
-	Cement leads in emission compliance, staying under its allowance.
-	Energy, Manufacturing, and Steel all exceed their allowances, indicating the need for intervention.
-	Manufacturing shows the highest compliance risk, with the largest emissions overshoot.
-	Overall industry trend: Most sectors are struggling to stay within their allocated emission budgets, signaling increasing operational or regulatory pressure.


## Net Emission Balance
-	Cement is the only sector with a positive net emission balance ,meaning it emitted less than its permitted allowance and shows strong compliance.
-	Energy, Manufacturing, and Steel all have negative balances, indicating they exceeded their emission allowances.
-	Manufacturing has the worst performance ,reflecting the largest gap between emissions and allowance.
-	Steel and Energy also show non-compliance, though to a lesser extent than Manufacturing.



## Carbon Credit Trading Behavior
-	Industries with Surplus: Steel, Cement
→ These industries maintain good emission efficiency and regularly sell excess credits.
-	Industries with Deficit: Energy, Manufacturing
→ These industries depend on buying credits, indicating higher emissions than allowed.
-	Steel has the strongest selling performance, while Energy has the highest credit demand.


## Net Trading Position
-	Steel and Cement show strong carbon performance, acting as net sellers of credits.
-	Energy and Manufacturing face large credit deficits, indicating higher emissions and reliance on purchased credits.
-	Energy is the worst performer with the largest deficit.
-	Steel is the best performer, followed by Cement.


## Total Market Value
-	Manufacturing and Steel dominate market value, reflecting larger activity in buying/selling credits.
-	Energy and Cement have comparatively lower market values in trading.

## Revenue vs Expenditure
-	Steel and Cement show strong carbon performance, acting as net sellers of credits, which gives them a positive balance.
-	Energy and Manufacturing have higher carbon expenditure than revenue, indicating they are net buyers and rely on purchasing credits.
-	Manufacturing has particularly high expenditure, reflecting its significant credit needs.
-	Steel achieves both relatively high revenue and a positive net balance, making it the best performer, while Energy is the worst due to the largest deficit.


## Cost Savings
-	Manufacturing achieves the highest cost savings, possibly from efficient credit utilization and strategic trading.
-	Steel also shows significant cost savings.
-	Energy and Cement show moderate to lower savings, linked to their trading and emission performance.




## FINAL CONCLUSION
Overall, the Carbon Credit Analysis (2024–2025) shows that:
-	Steel and Cement lead in carbon market performance, acting as net sellers of credits, achieving positive trading positions, and generating moderate to high revenue and cost savings indicating efficient emission management and profitable carbon trading.
-	Manufacturing has high carbon market activity and achieves the highest absolute cost savings, but it is a net buyer of credits due to high emissions, indicating room for improved emission control.
-	Energy faces the largest carbon credit deficit, with higher expenditure than revenue, making it the least efficient in carbon management, though it participates actively in trading.
-	Cement performs moderately in revenue and cost savings but relies on purchasing credits; it requires stronger emission reduction measures to improve net carbon performance.
